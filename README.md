## 2. Self-Funding Metrics

A self-funding economy can grow through two primary mechanisms:

1. **Recurring Cash Flow** — Generate a regular flow of revenue to fund ongoing operations and development.
2. **Growing Attention → Growing Consumption** — Attract increasing attention to products and services, leading to greater consumption and economic activity.

### Sources of Attention

Growing attention can come from:

- **A Single Product:** One successful product attracts users and drives consumption.
- **A Portfolio of Products:** Multiple products, applications, and content channels attract different audiences and create multiple sources of consumption.

### What We Measure

The goal is to understand how attention translates into consumption, how consumption generates value, and how that value can support further development.

## YouTube as Sample Example for Attention-Driven Self-Funded Economic Model

YouTube views are one way to measure attention, but they do not necessarily represent the actual impact of that attention.

For example, a video about a viral meme like Dogecoin may attract millions of views, while a video about a scientific problem like the Navier-Stokes equation may attract only a few thousand views. However, the latter could inspire researchers, attract developers, lead to new products, or encourage people to contribute to a project.

Since this model is intended to operate over a 10-year period, we need to measure not just the number of views but also the quality, persistence, and impact of the attention generated.

### 1. Attention, Consumption, and Cash Flow

The YouTube example can be divided into three layers.

#### Attention: What impact did we attract?

Measure the reach, quality, relevance, and influence of the attention generated.

Examples:
- Views and unique viewers
- Watch time and audience engagement
- Shares, mentions, and discussions
- Interest from researchers, developers, investors, and potential customers
- Attention that continues over months or years

#### Consumption: What did that attention lead people to do?

Measure the activities and usage that follow the initial attention.

Examples:
- Growth in channel views and returning viewers
- Visits to the project website
- Usage of AI applications such as Music AI
- Developer contributions and community participation
- Token swaps and other blockchain transactions

#### Cash Flow: What economic value did it generate?

Measure the actual revenue generated from the resulting consumption and economic activity.

Examples:
- YouTube advertising revenue
- Sponsorships
- Paid product usage
- Transaction fees
- Revenue from related products and services

### 2. Types of Attention Impact

View count alone cannot capture every aspect of attention. The following metrics can help measure its broader impact.

| Metric | What It Measures | YouTube Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | How many people encounter the content | Views and unique viewers |
| Depth of Attention | How deeply people engage with the content | Watch time, completion rate, and repeat viewing |
| Audience Quality | Whether the attention comes from relevant people | Researchers, developers, investors, and potential customers |
| Attention Propagation | Whether people spread the idea further | Shares, mentions, citations, remixes, and external links |
| Persistence | Whether attention continues over time | Views and discussions continuing months or years later |
| Influence and Impact | Whether the attention leads to meaningful outcomes | New projects, collaborations, integrations, and products |

These metrics help distinguish temporary viral attention from attention that continues to create value over time.

### 3. Comparing Two Types of YouTube Attention

Consider two hypothetical videos.

**Video A: A Viral Meme**
- 1,000,000 views
- Generates shares and online discussions
- Most viewers leave without taking further action
- Interest declines after a few weeks

**Video B: A Scientific Discovery**
- 5,000 views
- Attracts researchers and developers
- Leads to discussions, code contributions, and related applications
- Continues to generate interest and collaborations over several years

Video A performs better in terms of reach. Video B could have a greater long-term impact despite having far fewer views.

The objective is not to assume that low-view videos are always more valuable. It is to recognize that view count alone cannot determine the value of the attention generated.

### 4. Measuring Attention Over 10 Years

The model should track attention at two levels.

**Level A: Attention Generated**

Measure the reach, depth, audience quality, propagation, and persistence of attention.

**Level B: Outcomes Created by Attention**

Measure what happens as a result of that attention, including new users, developer contributions, product usage, collaborations, partnerships, and revenue generation.

Keeping these levels separate helps avoid counting the same outcome twice as both attention and consumption.

A 10-year dashboard could track:

- Annual Attention Impact Metrics
- Number of attention sources that continue attracting people
- Number of products, collaborations, and contributions resulting from that attention
- Consumption generated by those sources
- Revenue generated from that consumption
- Development costs and the proportion funded by project-generated revenue

Initially, these metrics can be tracked separately. As historical data accumulates, their relationship with future consumption and revenue can be analyzed to develop a weighted Attention Impact Score.

### 5. Tokenizing YouTube Views on Blockchain

Views can potentially be represented by tokens on a blockchain, creating a market around attention. However, token trading volume is not the same as revenue received by the project.

For example, when one person buys a token from another holder, the payment normally goes to the seller. The project earns revenue only when it receives value through mechanisms such as transaction fees or other revenue-generating activities.

Therefore, tokenized attention must be evaluated separately from the actual revenue it generates.

The economic model can be represented as:

**Attention → Consumption → Economic Activity → Revenue Received → Reinvestment**

### Conclusion

Attention should not be measured only by how many people watch something. It should also account for the relevance of the audience, how long the interest persists, whether the idea spreads, and what that attention enables people to do.

A viral meme, a scientific discovery, a developer project, a music video, and an AI application may attract very different audiences and view counts. Each can generate value through different forms of consumption.

The objective is to understand how these different sources of attention translate into consumption, how that consumption generates revenue, and how the resulting revenue can support continued development.

Over a 10-year period, this approach can help measure whether an attention-driven economic system is generating enough value to fund its own growth.
